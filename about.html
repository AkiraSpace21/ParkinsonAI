About this application

What it does

This application screens short voice recordings for acoustic patterns that research has associated with Parkinson's disease. You record or upload a sample of sustained speech, and the system returns a probability estimate along with the underlying acoustic measurements it computed.

How voice analysis works

Parkinson's disease can affect the muscles used for speech, which sometimes shows up as small, measurable changes in voice quality — reduced pitch variation, irregularity in vocal fold vibration, or a breathier tone. Acoustic analysis tools can pick up on some of these changes even when they're not obvious to the ear.

Acoustic features used

The screening model is built on features extracted directly from the recording:

  • Fundamental frequency (F0) — the perceived pitch of the voice, and how much it varies
  • Jitter — cycle-to-cycle variation in pitch period
  • Shimmer — cycle-to-cycle variation in amplitude
  • Harmonics-to-noise ratio (HNR) — the balance between periodic voice signal and noise
  • MFCCs and spectral descriptors — a broader representation of the voice's spectral shape, commonly used in speech analysis

How the screening model works

The extracted features are scaled using statistics from the model's training data, then passed to a trained classifier. The classifier outputs a probability that the recording's acoustic pattern is more consistent with recordings labeled Parkinson's than with recordings labeled healthy in its training data. A probability at or above 50% is reported as an elevated pattern indication; below that, as a typical pattern indication.

Limitations

  • This is a research screening tool, not a diagnostic instrument. A result in either direction is not a medical conclusion.
  • Model performance depends on the size and diversity of its training dataset. Results may be less reliable for voices, accents, or recording conditions not well represented in training.
  • Background noise, microphone quality, and recording length can all affect the accuracy of the extracted features.
  • The model was not validated as a clinical diagnostic device and has not undergone regulatory review.

Research purpose

This application exists to demonstrate an end-to-end voice screening pipeline — from audio capture through feature extraction to model inference — for research and academic purposes. It is not intended for clinical use or for making decisions about anyone's medical care.

Privacy

Recordings submitted for screening are processed to extract acoustic features and produce a result. Screening history shown in this application is held only in server memory for the current session and is cleared when the server restarts. Review your deployment's own data handling practices before using this application with recordings of real individuals.
